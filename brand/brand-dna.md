## 5. What we can NEVER say in Meta ads

Meta's Personal Health & Health/Fitness ad policies (and general Advertising Standards on unrealistic/misleading claims) apply directly to this product because it's marketed on weight/body-tone outcomes. Do not use, in any Meta (Facebook/Instagram) ad — copy, headline, image text, or video voiceover:

1. **No guaranteed or specific outcome claims.** Never say "you will lose weight," "lose X lbs in Y days/weeks," "guaranteed results," "guaranteed to tone." Keep to "may help," "can support," "designed to."
2. **No unqualified superlatives without backing on file.** "World's #1," "world's strongest," "best resistance leggings" — these appear in on-site product titles but should not be repeated in ad copy unless substantiation is filed and legal has signed off. Default to descriptive, non-superlative phrasing in ads ("high-resistance," "our strongest level").
3. **No overstating the science.** Never say "clinically proven," "doctor recommended," "Yale-approved," "medically proven," or imply Yale University or Yale School of Medicine endorses Skinnify as a brand/product. The correct framing is: "Inspired by findings from a study conducted at The John B. Pierce Laboratory, affiliated with Yale School of Medicine" — never shorten to "Yale-proven" or "proven by Yale."
4. **No disease, medical, or body-composition treatment claims.** Never say "burns fat," "melts fat," "cures," "treats," "prevents obesity," "reduces cellulite," "detoxes," "boosts metabolism" (unqualified), or anything implying a medical/therapeutic effect. This is apparel with embedded resistance, not a medical device or weight-loss drug.
5. **No personal-attribute targeting or body-shaming framing.** Never directly address the viewer's body, weight, or perceived flaws ("Tired of your belly fat?", "Hide those problem areas," "Finally fix your saggy [body part]"). Meta explicitly prohibits ads that imply or assert a personal health condition, body shape, or negative self-perception. Speak to the *lifestyle problem* (no time, no energy, gym doesn't fit), never to the *body*.
6. **No before/after body-transformation imagery presented as typical or guaranteed results** without a "results not typical" disclaimer, and never implying dramatic transformation from apparel alone.
7. **No implying this replaces diet, exercise, or medical treatment.** Never say "no need to exercise," "skip the gym forever and still get fit," or "works instead of dieting." Correct framing: it *adds* low-effort resistance to movement people already do — a complement to, not a replacement for, exercise/diet.
8. **No unverifiable stats stated as fact.** "150,000+ customers" and "94% success rate" need a verifiable source on file before use in paid ads; if unverifiable at time of ad creation, drop the stat or use directionally softer language ("thousands of customers").
9. **No injury/safety claims presented as medical fact.** "No injury risk" and "joint-friendly" are fine as comfort/design descriptors but must not be framed as medical/orthopedic guarantees (e.g., never "doctor-approved for joint pain" or "eliminates injury risk").
10. **No fake urgency/scarcity that isn't real** (e.g., fabricated countdowns, "only 3 left" if not tied to real inventory) — standard Meta misleading-claims policy.
11. **No implying FDA approval, clinical-trial status, or regulatory endorsement** of any kind — this product has none and needs none as apparel, but ad copy must never suggest otherwise.

**Default-safe substitution pattern for ads:** replace absolute claims ("burns 30% more calories") with study-anchored, qualified claims ("built-in resistance is designed to help you burn more calories during everyday movement, based on research from a Yale-affiliated lab — individual results vary").
