# Skinnify — Brand Voice

Derived from live copy on https://skinnify.co/ (homepage, collection pages, founder story, FAQ). Use this to keep ad copy, product copy, and creative scripts sounding like Skinnify.

## 1. Voice in one sentence

Skinnify talks like a smart, no-BS friend who gets that you're busy — warm and encouraging, never preachy, never gym-bro, never clinical. It sells *convenience and quiet consistency*, not intensity.

## 2. Tone attributes

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Warm, relatable, a little conspiratorial ("you already know the gym doesn't fit") | Cold, clinical, or corporate |
| Confident and matter-of-fact about benefits | Hyped-up, shouty, or "bro science" |
| Empowering ("reclaim your fitness") | Shaming or guilt-tripping |
| Simple, plain-spoken | Jargon-heavy or overly technical |
| Calm and steady ("small efforts, big results over time") | Urgent, panicked, or fear-based |
| Inclusive of real, busy lives (errands, coffee runs, work) | Aspirational-only / gym-influencer aesthetic |
| Backed by real research, cited plainly | Vague "science says" hand-waving |

## 3. Sentence & structure patterns (house style)

- **Short declaratives.** "Resistance, you can wear." "Comfort built for every day." Keep headlines to 3–6 words where possible.
- **Contrast/triad structure for benefits.** "No gym, no extra time, no sweat required." Three short parallel phrases, usually starting with "no" or a verb.
- **Second person, direct address.** "Tone your legs, glutes, and core with every step." Always "you," never "customers" or "users" in copy.
- **Everyday-life anchors, not gym anchors.** "Errand," "coffee run," "living your life," "frantic schedule" — ground benefits in ordinary moments, not workouts.
- **Quiet/compounding language for results.** "Every step adds up quietly, day after day" — results are steady and low-key, never dramatic overnight transformation.
- **Plain CTA verbs.** "Shop Now," "Shop Collection," "View all," "Read Full Study." No exclamation-point hype ("BUY NOW!!!").
- **Founder voice is personal and story-driven** when used (About/brand story placements) — first person, specific and emotional ("I watched my mom..."), never used in performance-ad copy verbatim without adaptation.

## 4. Do / Don't words

### Do use
- tone, toning, tones
- move, movement, moving
- built-in, engineered, designed
- everyday, daily life, real life
- effortless, quiet, steady, small efforts
- reclaim, fit into your life
- resistance (as the hero mechanic/ingredient)
- comfort, comfortable, breathable
- busy, frantic schedule, no extra time
- results over time, add up
- research-backed / study-backed (with attribution)
- "may help," "designed to," "can support" (for outcome claims)

### Don't use
- melt (fat), burn fat (unqualified), torch calories
- guarantee(d) results, instant results, overnight
- cure, treat, fix, detox
- lazy girl hack, hack your body
- "no effort at all" / "do nothing and still get fit"
- gym-bro jargon: shredded, jacked, gains, swole
- shame-adjacent words: flabby, saggy, problem areas, hide your [body part]
- clinically proven (without qualification), doctor-recommended, Yale-approved
- "world's #1" / "world's strongest" in ad copy without legal sign-off (fine as on-site product-title flavor, not for ads — see brand-dna.md §5)
- desperate urgency language: "last chance," fabricated countdowns
- overly technical/clinical terms (e.g., "biomechanical load," "eccentric loading") — keep language plain

## 5. Voice checks — before/after examples

| Off-brand (too hyped / too clinical / non-compliant) | On-brand rewrite |
|---|---|
| "MELT FAT INSTANTLY with our miracle leggings!!" | "Turn your daily walk into a toning session — no gym required." |
| "Clinically proven to burn 30% more calories, guaranteed." | "Built-in resistance is designed to help you burn more calories as you move, based on research from a Yale-affiliated lab." |
| "Tired of hiding your belly? Fix it with Skinnify." | "Most of us don't have an extra hour for the gym. Skinnify turns the time you already have into progress." |
| "Buy now before it's too late!!!" | "Shop the collection." |
| "Doctors recommend Skinnify for joint pain." | "Joint-friendly by design — comfortable enough for every day." |

## 6. Formatting conventions

- Product names are Title Case ("Resistance Band Leggings," "Weighted Power Sleeve").
- Benefit lists favor short parallel fragments over full sentences (see homepage bullet style: "Tone legs, glutes & core / Lose weight / Burn more calories / No gym, no extra time").
- Numbers are used sparingly and always tied to something verifiable (band count, price, return window) — not vague superlative stats invented for effect.
- Avoid emojis and heavy exclamation punctuation; the brand's confidence comes from plain statements, not punctuation intensity.
