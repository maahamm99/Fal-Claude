## ICP #1 — The Time-Starved Multitasker (primary persona)

**Who she is:** Woman, roughly late 20s–40s, working full-time and/or managing a household or kids. Disposable income exists but discretionary *time* does not. Has tried gym memberships before (guilt-purchased, underused).

**Core pain points:**
- No time for a structured workout routine — the gym is logistically impossible most weeks.
- Guilt/frustration over unused gym memberships or abandoned fitness apps.
- Wants to look and feel toned but isn't willing to sacrifice more hours she doesn't have.
- Fatigue with "all-or-nothing" fitness culture that demands intensity she can't sustain.

**Voice-of-customer phrases `[INFERRED from FAQ/site framing]`:**
- "I don't have an extra hour to work out every day."
- "I just want something I can wear and forget about."
- "Will this actually do anything, or is it just leggings?"
- "I've tried everything and nothing fits into my schedule."
- "I want to feel like I'm doing *something* even on my busiest days."

**Objections & how the brand answers them:**
| Objection | Brand's answer (from site) |
|---|---|
| "This sounds like a gimmick — does built-in resistance actually work?" | Study citation: John B. Pierce Laboratory (Yale-affiliated) research on resistance-band calorie burn; framed as "based on research," not a guarantee. |
| "Will I actually notice a difference if I'm not sweating/working out?" | "Small efforts. Big results over time" — reframes success as compounding, not immediate/dramatic. |
| "Is this just expensive leggings?" | Product ladder (4/6/8/10-band tiers) + non-legging products (pants, jumpsuit, weighted sleeve, shoes) shows it's a system, not a single item. |
| "What if it doesn't work for me?" | 14-day money-back return policy, full refund. |

## ICP #2 — The Reluctant/Recovering Gym-Goer

**Who she is:** Has a complicated relationship with traditional fitness — previously gym-going but burned out, injured, self-conscious in gym settings, or simply over the "peaks and crashes" of intense workout cycles. Values privacy and low-pressure movement over performance culture.

**Core pain points:**
- Burnout from high-intensity routines that aren't sustainable long-term.
- Discomfort with public gym environments (self-consciousness, body image).
- Wants low-impact, joint-friendly ways to stay active without re-injury risk.
- Craves consistency without the emotional highs/crashes of "workout mode."

**Voice-of-customer phrases `[INFERRED]`:**
- "I don't want to go back to the gym and burn out again."
- "I need something gentle on my joints."
- "I like that no one can tell I'm 'working out.'"
- "I want consistency, not another crash-and-quit cycle."

**Objections & how the brand answers them:**
| Objection | Brand's answer (from site) |
|---|---|
| "Will this hurt my joints or cause injury like intense workouts did?" | Positioned as "joint-friendly," "no injury risk" — low-impact by design. |
| "I don't want people to know / see me 'trying.'" | "Total privacy" — you can wear it under regular clothes, resistance is invisible to others. |
| "I've quit fitness routines before — why would this be different?" | "Without the peaks and crashes of traditional workouts" — framed explicitly against burnout-prone fitness culture. |
| "Can I still use it if I do go to the gym sometimes?" | FAQ directly addresses "Can I wear these products to the gym?" — product is compatible with, not exclusive of, traditional workouts. |

## ICP #3 — The Practical, Value-Conscious Shopper

**Who she is:** Comparison-shops before buying, skeptical of DTC fitness-apparel hype, reads FAQs and return policies closely before purchasing. Price-sensitive relative to the $70–$150 range, wants proof the spend is justified (durability, real function, not just marketing).

**Core pain points:**
- Skepticism toward "as seen on Instagram" fitness gadgets that overpromise.
- Concern about product durability and longevity of embedded bands/weights.
- Needs practical reassurance: care instructions, fit, versatility, return terms.
- Wants confirmation the product functions as *normal, wearable clothing* first, resistance gear second.

**Voice-of-customer phrases `[INFERRED from FAQ topics]`:**
- "Do the bands lose their strength or break over time?"
- "Can I just throw these in the wash like normal leggings?"
- "Are these actually squat-proof and non-see-through?"
- "What's the actual difference between this and regular leggings — am I paying extra for nothing?"
- "Can I mix and match pieces, or do I need the whole set?"

**Objections & how the brand answers them:**
| Objection | Brand's answer (from site) |
|---|---|
| "Will the bands wear out?" | FAQ directly covers this topic ("Do the resistance bands or weights ever break or lose their strength over time?") — reassurance built into pre-purchase content. |
| "Are these normal enough to wear daily, not just as gym gear?" | "Comfort built for every day," breathable fabric, styled in everyday lifestyle colors (not just performance black). |
| "Is this worth $70–$150 vs. $20 leggings?" | Differentiated by embedded resistance mechanic + tiered system (4/6/8/10 bands) + multi-category product line (leggings, pants, tops, shoes) that regular leggings don't offer. |
| "What if quality doesn't match the price?" | 14-day return policy removes purchase risk. |

## Cross-ICP notes for ad targeting

- **Common thread across all three personas:** the enemy is not "fitness" — it's *time scarcity, burnout, and gym friction*. Every ICP responds to "fits into your life" more than "get fit."
- **Sizing signal:** XS–4XL range on the core leggings line indicates the brand targets a broad, inclusive body-size audience, not a narrow fitness-enthusiast niche — avoid narrow-body-type imagery/casting in ad creative.
- **Do not conflate personas in copy.** ICP #2's "low-impact/recovering" framing and ICP #1's "no time at all" framing pull in different emotional directions (relief from intensity vs. relief from time pressure) — pick one angle per ad, don't mix messages.
- **Never turn objections into shame hooks.** E.g., ICP #3's skepticism about durability should be met with reassurance (FAQ-style proof), never with copy that mocks or pressures the skeptic.
